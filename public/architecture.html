A second opinion inside the banking app

Second Look is proposed as a widget embedded in an existing payment review. The host bank keeps authentication, payment confirmation and account authority.

Architecture proposal. The current prototype uses scripted replies, simulated flags, local case records and demo freezes. It is not a deployable ML service or production widget SDK.

When the system can act

  • Normal: keep the existing payment review. Assistance remains available.
  • Concern: explain specific reason codes and offer optional chat. A member who skips remains eligible for respectful review.
  • Critical: a bank-approved policy can request a reversible payment hold after corroborating signals. The bank backend enforces it, shows its scope and expiry, and provides review or appeal. Closing chat does not release the hold.
  • Account scope: a full account restriction requires separate bank authorization and an approved recovery procedure. A model score alone does not grant that authority.

How the widget integrates

The banking app creates a payment intent, then its backend obtains a member-scoped widget session. Second Look receives an opaque payment reference and approved reason codes. It emits assistance, support and review events; it cannot confirm, release or freeze real payments directly. For native apps, a secure web view or native wrapper would be required and validated with Sound’s platform team.

ML development and rollout

Train on labeled historical data under bank approval; evaluate on later, member-separated records, including legitimate look-alikes. Calibrate scores, measure recall at an acceptable false-positive rate, and compare against simple rules. Start in shadow mode, then pilot explanations and human review before enabling narrowly scoped holds. Synthetic hackathon examples cannot establish real-world fraud performance.

Privacy and operating limits

Use only authorized bank events and member-volunteered answers. Do not assume access to private calls, texts, email or voice-cloning detection. Use staff roles, minimal retention, secure contact enrollment, audit records and an incident rollback path. Define what happens when ML is unavailable; the conversation must not invent a score or change payment status.
